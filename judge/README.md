L1 — sealed judge (brief 1); released as a digest-pinned image at L2. So far: the OCI runtime abstraction (`runtime.py`, brief 1 §2 and A1 item 2). It builds the one command line that runs a candidate container (no `--rm`: removal is the judge's explicit act): `--network=none`, `--read-only` (and Podman's `--read-only-tmpfs=false`, so both runtimes give the same root), `--cap-drop=ALL`, `--security-opt=no-new-privileges`, no mount of any host path, and CPU, memory (swap equal, so none) and process limits from `config.toml`. Docker is admitted only if it reports itself as Docker with a server; Podman only if it is rootless with the cpu, memory and pids controllers delegated. P2 (`harness/pins.py`) runs a probe in that container under every usable runtime and requires at least one. Each denial must be an OS errno: ENOENT or EACCES/EPERM for judge paths, ENETUNREACH for IPv4 and IPv6 TCP and UDP, EROFS for the root. The probe must also see only loopback and the configured cgroup limits. The episode loop (`episodes.py`, brief 1 §2): JSON-RPC 2.0 over the container's stdio, one message per line; per step the judge sends `{t, observation, legal_actions}` and advances the World only on the reply. Every reply is bounded by `[timeouts] step_s` and every episode by `episode_s` from `config.toml`, and a reply line by `reply_bytes`. Every episode is recorded with a termination code: `terminal`/`truncated` when the World ends it; `crash`, `step_timeout`, `episode_timeout`, `invalid_reply`, `illegal_action` when the candidate fails it, after which the container is torn down and the next episode starts in a fresh one. Teardown is `rm -f` (Podman with `--time=0`, so SIGKILL at once), and then the runtime's listing must name nothing; a container still listed is termination `leaked`, counted. P3 (survivorship) and P10 (teardown) run under every usable runtime. Prediction scoring (`scoring.py`, brief 1 §2): a step reply's prediction naming an undeclared target, of the wrong length, or not summing to one within `len(support)` machine epsilons (a derived bound) ends the episode `invalid_reply` before the World advances, as does a reply carrying NaN or an infinity, which is not JSON. Each declared target is then scored against the World's next observation of its field as −ln of the probability on the realised value: zero scores +inf, counted and never clipped; an omitted target is missing, never imputed. Each episode record carries per target `scored`, `missing`, `infinite`, `finite_sum` and `log_loss` (the finite sum, or the string `+inf`, since JSON has no infinity), with `scored + missing` equal to the episode's steps. P7 witnesses it under every usable runtime. P27 pins the sum bound itself, for every support size a bound World declares: a sum 2·`len(support)` epsilons from one is invalid, one `len(support)` epsilons away and a softmax normalised by naive summation in adversarial order are scored (brief 2 A2, L2a obligation 10; mutants `tolerance_loose`, `tolerance_zero`). Measured compute (`compute.py`, brief 1 §2): each episode record carries `compute` with `cpu_s`, `wall_s`, `peak_memory_bytes` and `unmeasured_s`, read by the judge from the cgroup the runtime created for the container (found through the container's init process in `/proc` and cut at the component naming the container's id), never from the candidate. The container is sampled after every reply and at the episode's end; its CPU time is partitioned over its episodes, each charged from the previous episode's last sample (or the container's start) to its own. `peak_memory_bytes` is the container's `memory.peak` to that sample. A crashed container takes its cgroup with it: `unmeasured_s` is the wall-clock from the last sample read to the episode's end, and the CPU spent in it is at most the configured CPUs times it. P4 witnesses it under every usable runtime. Compute priced (`price.py`, brief 1 A4 item 4): each record carries `price` {`cpu`, `memory`, `total`} in the World's utility at the rates its manifest's `price` block commits, with their derivation in its `rationale` (Crafter: an episode exhausting every allowance costs 0.5 achievements, half per resource); a manifest committing no price has none, and `evaluate()` refuses its World `unpriced` before any seed is issued, never borrowing another World's rates (brief 2 A2, P20), with `compute.peak_memory_scope` = `container_since_start` stating the peak's scope; memory is that peak over the measured stretch, and the unmeasured stretch is charged at the configured CPUs and memory. P12 witnesses it under every usable runtime. Calibration (`scoring.py`, brief 1 A4 item 3): each record carries `calibration` per declared target, the predicted entropies' `entropy_sum` and `mean_entropy` in nats (null for a target never predicted, never imputed) and a `reliability` table over the declared support: per support value and per bin of predicted probability (`[calibration] bins` of `config.toml`, equal bins of [0, 1], the last closed at 1), `n` steps, `hits` that realised the value and `p_sum` of the probabilities. Every scored step, an infinite-loss step included, enters every row once, so each row's `n` sums to `scored`; tables add across episodes and are never pooled across targets. P13 witnesses it; P7 checks the recorded calibration of its `forecaster` episode under every usable runtime. Seed issuance (`seeds.py`, brief 1 section 2): evaluation seeds are drawn from `secrets` over Crafter's seed range and keyed by the seed the World actually generates from (`binding.world_seed`: Crafter reduces `hash((seed, 1))` modulo 2**31 - 1, so distinct seeds can name one World). Odd keys are the evaluation domain, even keys the public training stream (`training_seeds`, SHA-256 of a tag, the World and a counter, filtered to even keys), so the two are disjoint by construction and the disjointness is checked. `admit` is the issuance check: under an exclusive lock on the World's append-only store in a 0700 judge-private directory it rereads every record and refuses, recording nothing, a seed keyed like any seed issued before or another in its request, a training-domain key, a seed outside the range, or a reused request id; `issue` returns only admitted seeds. P1 witnesses it. Mutants: `seed_reissued` (the evaluator reissues used seeds; the check refuses them), `reuse_check_skipped`, `domain_check_skipped`, `training_undomained`. Each seed record states `key_version`, the interpreter (implementation and version) that computed its key, and `admit` refuses `key_version`, recording nothing, when any earlier record states another or none, since keys are not comparable across interpreters (brief 2 A2, L2a obligation 8; P26, mutant `key_version_ignored`). Tamper-evident ledger (`ledger.py`, brief 1 A4 item 1 and A5 F4): `append(ledger, private, request_id, records)` writes a request's records to append-only JSONL, opened `O_APPEND` and never seeked, each carrying `request_id`, `prev_sha256` and `sha256` (the SHA-256 of its canonical serialisation, sorted keys and no whitespace, without its own `sha256`); a request's first record chains from the SHA-256 of its `request_id` as UTF-8 bytes. After each request the judge appends the request's head (ledger name, `request_id`, record count, last `sha256`, ledger length in bytes) to `heads.jsonl` in its private storage, signed by HMAC-SHA256 under a key generated there on first start and never exported; a `request_id` already written is refused. `python -m judge verify LEDGER PRIVATE ANCHOR` recomputes every chain and checks every signature, and exits 0 only if the ledger is exactly the signed requests, in order, and ANCHOR is one of their heads. Each signed head stands alone, so the ledger and `heads.jsonl` cannot commit to how many requests there were; the caller holds that commitment: the anchor is the last `sha256` of a request it was returned, and every request up to it is committed, later ones admitted (review 2817eb7 F2; the pilot anchors at its last record). P9 witnesses it, the last request truncated with its head and both files deleted included. Mutant `independent_heads` ignores the anchor. Proposer from the registering credential (`registry.py`, brief 1 A4 item 2): `python -m judge credential PRIVATE KIND MODEL SESSION` issues a credential to one party of CLAUDE.md A1.7 (`kind` builder, reviewer, guy or counsel; model and session required except for guy) and prints its 64-hex token once; the judge keeps only the token's SHA-256 beside the party in `credentials.jsonl` in its 0700 private storage. `python -m judge register PRIVATE IMAGE` reads the token from stdin, never the command line, and registers the image digest (`sha256:<64 hex>`) to the token's holder in `registrations.jsonl`; registration reads nothing of the image but its digest, so a candidate cannot name its own proposer. A token matching no issued credential (none, forged, a proposer the candidate names, the stored hash of a real token) is refused `unattributed`, recording nothing; an image registered by one party is refused `registered` to any other, and the same party registering again changes nothing. `registry.proposer_of(private, image)` is the `proposer` every judge record of the image carries. P16 witnesses it. `evaluate()` (`evaluate.py`, brief 1 section 2): `evaluate(image, world, n_episodes, request_id, private=, ledgers=, runtime_name=)`, or `python -m judge evaluate PRIVATE LEDGERS RUNTIME IMAGE WORLD N REQUEST_ID`, which prints the request's records as JSON lines. It refuses an image never registered (`unregistered`) before anything else, so no seed is issued to it. A `request_id` the World's ledger (`LEDGERS/<world>.ledger.jsonl`) holds under a signed head returns its records as written, running and issuing nothing, once they are checked against that head (signature, count, chain, last `sha256`; a mismatch, or a heads file or ledger that is not JSONL, is refused `ledger_unverified`; brief 2 A2, L2a obligation 3, P22); asked of another image, World or episode count it is refused `request_conflict`; one whose seeds were issued but whose records were never signed is refused `interrupted`, its seeds spent. Each record is appended to the ledger as its episode ends (`episodes.played` yields it once final; `ledger.append` writes as it draws), and every request takes the World's lock in PRIVATE and first seals each interrupted request of the World (`ledger.seal_interrupted`): its played episodes and one closing record stating `interrupted`, seeds `issued`, the `image` and `proposer` they were issued to (each seed record in PRIVATE carries both from issue, review 0a2c02c F2) and episodes `recorded`, signed. A request killed and asked again under another id thus leaves its played episodes and spent seeds on the record (brief 2 A2, L2a obligation 4, review 6debf231b630 F3; P23, mutants `partial_dropped`, `interrupted_unsealed`; P3 mutant `played_at_end`). The judge binds every directory of `worlds/` holding both a `manifest.json` and a `binding.py`, under the directory's name, and keeps no table of Worlds (`evaluate.bound`; brief 2 A2, P19). An unknown World or runtime, an unpriced World, a non-positive count, an unusable runtime or an image not present locally is refused before any seed is issued; the judge never pulls a candidate. Otherwise it issues `n_episodes` fresh seeds (`seeds.issue`, in PRIVATE), builds one World per seed only as the loop reaches it, plays them through `episodes.run`, stamps each record with `schema_version`, `judge_sha256` (SHA-256 over every file of `judge/`, `worlds/` and `uv.lock` but bytecode caches, in path order: path, NUL, the file's SHA-256, newline), `image`, `proposer` (`proposer_of` the image), `world`, `runtime`, `n_episodes` and the episode's `achievements`, and appends them through `ledger.append`. No record carries a seed. P17 witnesses it under every usable runtime. Mutants: `fixed_proposer`, `seeds_before_proposer`, `idempotency_dropped`, `conflict_unchecked`, `hash_partial`.
