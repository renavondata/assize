L1 — sealed judge (brief 1); released as a digest-pinned image at L2. So far: the OCI runtime abstraction (`runtime.py`, brief 1 §2 and A1 item 2). It builds the one command line that runs a candidate container (no `--rm`: removal is the judge's explicit act): `--network=none`, `--read-only` (and Podman's `--read-only-tmpfs=false`, so both runtimes give the same root), `--cap-drop=ALL`, `--security-opt=no-new-privileges`, no mount of any host path, and CPU, memory (swap equal, so none) and process limits from `config.toml`. Docker is admitted only if it reports itself as Docker with a server; Podman only if it is rootless with the cpu, memory and pids controllers delegated. P2 (`harness/pins.py`) runs a probe in that container under every usable runtime and requires at least one. Each denial must be an OS errno: ENOENT or EACCES/EPERM for judge paths, ENETUNREACH for IPv4 and IPv6 TCP and UDP, EROFS for the root. The probe must also see only loopback and the configured cgroup limits. The episode loop (`episodes.py`, brief 1 §2): JSON-RPC 2.0 over the container's stdio, one message per line; per step the judge sends `{t, observation, legal_actions}` and advances the World only on the reply. Every reply is bounded by `[timeouts] step_s` and every episode by `episode_s` from `config.toml`, and a reply line by `reply_bytes`. Every episode is recorded with a termination code: `terminal`/`truncated` when the World ends it; `crash`, `step_timeout`, `episode_timeout`, `invalid_reply`, `illegal_action` when the candidate fails it, after which the container is torn down and the next episode starts in a fresh one. Teardown is `rm -f` (Podman with `--time=0`, so SIGKILL at once), and then the runtime's listing must name nothing; a container still listed is termination `leaked`, counted. P3 (survivorship) and P10 (teardown) run under every usable runtime. Prediction scoring (`scoring.py`, brief 1 §2): a step reply's prediction naming an undeclared target, of the wrong length, or not summing to one within `len(support)` machine epsilons (a derived bound) ends the episode `invalid_reply` before the World advances, as does a reply carrying NaN or an infinity, which is not JSON. Each declared target is then scored against the World's next observation of its field as −ln of the probability on the realised value: zero scores +inf, counted and never clipped; an omitted target is missing, never imputed. Each episode record carries per target `scored`, `missing`, `infinite`, `finite_sum` and `log_loss` (the finite sum, or the string `+inf`, since JSON has no infinity), with `scored + missing` equal to the episode's steps. P7 witnesses it under every usable runtime. Measured compute (`compute.py`, brief 1 §2): each episode record carries `compute` with `cpu_s`, `wall_s`, `peak_memory_bytes` and `unmeasured_s`, read by the judge from the cgroup the runtime created for the container (found through the container's init process in `/proc` and cut at the component naming the container's id), never from the candidate. The container is sampled after every reply and at the episode's end; its CPU time is partitioned over its episodes, each charged from the previous episode's last sample (or the container's start) to its own. `peak_memory_bytes` is the container's `memory.peak` to that sample. A crashed container takes its cgroup with it: `unmeasured_s` is the wall-clock from the last sample read to the episode's end, and the CPU spent in it is at most the configured CPUs times it. P4 witnesses it under every usable runtime. Compute priced (`price.py`, brief 1 A4 item 4): each record carries `price` {`cpu`, `memory`, `total`} in the World's utility at the rates `[price.<world>]` of `config.toml` (Crafter: an episode exhausting every allowance costs 0.5 achievements, half per resource), with `compute.peak_memory_scope` = `container_since_start` stating the peak's scope; memory is that peak over the measured stretch, and the unmeasured stretch is charged at the configured CPUs and memory. P12 witnesses it under every usable runtime. Calibration (`scoring.py`, brief 1 A4 item 3): each record carries `calibration` per declared target, the predicted entropies' `entropy_sum` and `mean_entropy` in nats (null for a target never predicted, never imputed) and a `reliability` table over the declared support: per support value and per bin of predicted probability (`[calibration] bins` of `config.toml`, equal bins of [0, 1], the last closed at 1), `n` steps, `hits` that realised the value and `p_sum` of the probabilities. Every scored step, an infinite-loss step included, enters every row once, so each row's `n` sums to `scored`; tables add across episodes and are never pooled across targets. P13 witnesses it; P7 checks the recorded calibration of its `forecaster` episode under every usable runtime. Seed issuance (`seeds.py`, brief 1 section 2): evaluation seeds are drawn from `secrets` over Crafter's seed range and keyed by the seed the World actually generates from (`binding.world_seed`: Crafter reduces `hash((seed, 1))` modulo 2**31 - 1, so distinct seeds can name one World). Odd keys are the evaluation domain, even keys the public training stream (`training_seeds`, SHA-256 of a tag, the World and a counter, filtered to even keys), so the two are disjoint by construction and the disjointness is checked. `admit` is the issuance check: under an exclusive lock on the World's append-only store in a 0700 judge-private directory it rereads every record and refuses, recording nothing, a seed keyed like any seed issued before or another in its request, a training-domain key, a seed outside the range, or a reused request id; `issue` returns only admitted seeds. P1 witnesses it. Mutants: `seed_reissued` (the evaluator reissues used seeds; the check refuses them), `reuse_check_skipped`, `domain_check_skipped`, `training_undomained`. Tamper-evident ledger (`ledger.py`, brief 1 A4 item 1 and A5 F4): `append(ledger, private, request_id, records)` writes a request's records to append-only JSONL, opened `O_APPEND` and never seeked, each carrying `request_id`, `prev_sha256` and `sha256` (the SHA-256 of its canonical serialisation, sorted keys and no whitespace, without its own `sha256`); a request's first record chains from the SHA-256 of its `request_id` as UTF-8 bytes. After each request the judge appends the request's head (ledger name, `request_id`, record count, last `sha256`, ledger length in bytes) to `heads.jsonl` in its private storage, signed by HMAC-SHA256 under a key generated there on first start and never exported; a `request_id` already written is refused. `python -m judge verify LEDGER PRIVATE` recomputes every chain and checks every signature, and exits 0 only if the ledger is exactly the signed requests, in order. P9 witnesses it. `evaluate()` (brief 1 section 2) is not yet built; it will write its records through `append`.
