L1 — pilot and time-to-learn (brief 1 §5). Host only: nothing here is evidence when run in the cloud.

`config.toml` commits, before any scored run, the pilot (200 incumbent episodes of Crafter or 2 hours, whichever binds first; reaching the cap first is a stop-and-report boundary), the assumed shifts δ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1}, the posterior mass 0.9, the FIT (δ = 0.5) and MARGINAL (δ = 1) criteria over a one-day budget, and the analysis's Monte Carlo sizes and seed. None is tuned after a score is seen.

`timetolearn.py` takes the pilot's per-episode utilities and its measured throughput (episodes per wall-clock second). Assumption, stated in every report: a candidate is distributed as the incumbent shifted by δ. The incumbent's distribution has the Bayesian-bootstrap posterior over the pilot's utilities; an experiment of m episodes per arm draws a distribution from it, m incumbent and m shifted episodes, and the Bayesian-bootstrap posterior of each arm's mean; the detection measure at m is the posterior mass on candidate mean > incumbent mean, averaged over experiments (the preposterior probability that the favoured sign is right). `table` reports per δ the smallest m reaching 0.9, searched up to the m the budget affords with both arms run, and its wall-clock 2m / throughput; `verdict` reads FIT, MARGINAL or UNFIT off the table. That is the expected reading of "90% posterior mass on the correct sign", and it gives the verdict; beside it the table carries the assurance reading, the smallest m at which the mass reaches 0.9 in 90% of experiments (about twice as many episodes), with its wall-clock, and `verdict(rows, c, assured=True)` reads the other verdict (review 0d5e9ec F1). Each reading is a Monte Carlo mean on its own stream per m, so it is not monotone in m: the search reads every m from 1, and each row carries the reading's standard error at m*, the reading and its error at m* − 1, and the same at the budget. A δ's detection is settled (`settled`) only if the reading at the budget lies more than one standard error from 0.9 on the side of the detection; `within_error` names the δs the verdict reads that are not, and the report marks such a verdict as within Monte Carlo error (review 0d5e9ec F2). A random agent's variance is a weak proxy for a competent agent's.

    uv run --frozen python -c "from pilot import timetolearn as t; c = t.config()['time_to_learn']; rows = t.table(UTILITIES, THROUGHPUT, c); print(rows, t.verdict(rows, c))"

P15 (`harness/pins.py`) pins it.

`run.py` and `python -m pilot` run the pilot through `evaluate()` (brief 1 §2): the random incumbent's image is built offline, its proposer (`[incumbent]`, the `Proposer:` trailer of 0f698c4, which added it) is issued a credential and the image registered under it, and the committed episodes are asked for in requests of `[requests] episodes`, the cap checked before each. The report (`report.json`, `report.md`) carries the verdict first, then the records' proposer, judge hash and image, every episode's utility and termination, Hafner's score beside the utility, the predictive floor's log-loss and calibration per target, compute per episode, throughput over the pilot's whole wall-clock and the time-to-learn table with its assumption. Reaching the cap, a refused or short request, or records of more than one proposer, judge hash or image stop it with no verdict. P18 pins it over a faked `evaluate()`.
